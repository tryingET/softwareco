## Lenses

1. customer and problem evidence;
2. outcome ownership and decision rights;
3. portfolio and capacity economics.

## Strengths

- Internal operating incoherence is credibly evidenced.
- The RFC distinguishes customer outcome from deployment.
- It requires accountable ownership, finite commitment, and stop/redirect choices.
- It avoids pretending that stewardship authority already exists.

## Must-fix findings

1. Select or constrain the first pilot enough to identify its customer/internal-user segment, problem, workaround, and harmed outcome.
2. Add direct evidence linking one broken demand-to-outcome journey to delay, waste, reliability loss, or unmet need.
3. Pre-register baseline, target, horizon, guardrails, evidence source, and stop/redirect threshold.
4. Identify who appoints and removes the pilot steward, outcome owner, and product/service owner.
5. Add a decision-rights matrix for intake, commitment, capacity displacement, promotion, incident override, outcome acceptance, stop/redirect, and retirement.
6. Define disagreement and escalation rules across portfolio, outcome, product/service, and runtime owners.
7. Define a portfolio comparison rule that includes expected value, urgency/cost of delay, risk, effort/capacity, confidence, and displaced alternatives.
