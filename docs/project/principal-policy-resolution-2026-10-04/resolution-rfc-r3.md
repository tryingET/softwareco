# Principal policy settlement — R3

## 1. Decision requested and contract boundary

Accept P01–P09 below and the expressly incorporated [contract appendix](contracts-r3.md)
after actual Tier-1 inspection and owner acceptance. This is a **SoftwareCo policy
proposal**, not a global semantic change, appointment, template implementation,
birth permit, memory runtime or production activation.

Primary driver: the operator needs a standing Principal who initiates and completes
useful technical outcomes without repeated routine approval requests. Stable core:
identity, decision rights, owner composition, one-attempt birth and truthful evidence.
Adapters: owner-native record lookup, admission/enforcement, template rendering,
provider/retrieval/storage and host execution. No new sovereign authority database
or schema is established by the logical contracts.

Critical invariants: persona/history never grants authority; source owners retain
their rights; classification cannot be caller-forged; revocation blocks new admissions;
one O3-issued exception start right admits at most one attempt across ALL permit IDs;
partial/unknown effects remain explicit;
inert birth is not active agency. Main risks are ambiguous ownership, uncontrolled
configuration, repeated birth after interruption and falsely complete qualification.

This R3 is a new exact review subject. [R1](resolution-rfc.md), [R2](resolution-rfc-r2.md)
and their output manifests remain historical. Both preceding reviews were **author
self-inspections**, not independent closure. [R3 evidence/dispositions](evidence-r3.md)
addresses the targeted R2 findings; [reviewer answers](reviewer-answers-r3.md) supplies
the four current answers through many-of-the-greats. [Review plan](review-set-plan-r3.md)
is planned, not executed. R2's earlier five answers remain historical rationale.

## 2. Evidence-backed scope of the conflict

- D68's accepted RFC already required main-first agent output. Current MR-only source
  prose is a **conformance defect**, not a new workflow waiver.
- D68's new-production-L2 restriction still requires an applicable accepted template-
  owner disposition. D167 establishes birth architecture, not automatic appointment
  or this actor's freeze exception.
- D167's target is company-pinned L0 birth; current copies are transitional. AK6350
  lineage is done and AK6351 birth work is pending on the scoped readback.
- Registry source includes the selected SoftwareCo path. Fleet-policy admission,
  installed discovery and lint compatibility remain separate checks.
- Advisory default and standing appointment are compatible only when **owner-issued
  permissions**, not persona text, supply the additional authority.

No generic template refresh, full fleet migration, language winner, universal memory
or live XENO is a demonstrated prerequisite to an inert identity scaffold.

## 3. Three independent dimensions of the design

| Dimension | Chosen position | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Target architecture** | standing owner-identified actor; one persona source repository; owner-native authority; one host executor; D167 pinned-L0 births | appointment issuance, implemented owner APIs, actual confinement or recovery |
| **Transitional compatibility** | existing legacy lineage readers remain; current company copies may stay until their ordered retirement; new Principal birth defaults to qualified D167 path | automatic legacy-copy birth permission or every old instruction becoming valid |
| **Migration mechanics** | correct canonical source/tests; govern the company's exact pin/adoption; qualify one selected birth route; issue exact one-attempt permit; render/verify inert repository | global copy removal, fleet rollout, active host or private recall |

**Preferred route A:** qualify AK6351's D167 birth path plus the necessary source-
conformance changes and exact company pin/adoption. Do not require AK6352/6353 to
finish across every company before this birth.

**Strongest bounded alternative B:** a separately admitted transitional birth from
an exact company copy, proved equivalent to its recorded pinned source and accepted
by lineage/refresh readers. This is not permanent copy ownership. It needs an explicit
owner exception naming route B, exact bytes, expiry/consumption and support contract;
this RFC **does not activate B**. Compare actual qualification/maintenance cost and
receiver opportunity cost, not invented deadlines. Choose B only if A cannot yet
qualify a receiver-valued result and B's complete contract is demonstrably admissible.

A is preferred because it implements already accepted D167 direction and avoids
qualifying two birth/refresh routes for the same actor. There is insufficient evidence
for a numerical speed advantage of either route.

## 4. Authority composition — not one signature for all owners

The [owner/action matrix](contracts-r3.md#owner-composition) distinguishes policy
acceptance, appointment issuance, birth permission and implementation/adoption.

- The SoftwareCo policy/appointment decider must be established through a current
  owner-issued record. Historical human-operator/Org Owner references identify the
  intended decider; expired D68/D74 activation is not current proof.
- P05 requires the applicable **template authority's accepted D68 disposition**.
  D173's company scope cannot alone provide another owner's approval.
- L0 changes, company pin/adoption and fleet placement/discovery changes require
  their actual owners' exact scopes and receipts. Approval of company direction
  is not permission to mutate global L0 or Holding semantics.
- No memory/profile/host approval is inferred from accepting an inert-birth policy.

The same human can hold multiple owner roles, but the acts, subjects and capacities
must be attributable separately. A generic “approved” field cannot fill every slot.
An applicable current standing owner agreement may supply a slot; do not require a
new per-task signature where that agreement already grants it. Missing or disputed
owner authority blocks only the dependent act, not unaffected discovery.

## 5. Operative clauses

### P01 — actor, role and repository

The Principal is an enduring actor identified by an accepted owner reference. The
canonical System Architect **role type**, its individual role appointment, session
occurrences and authenticated security principals have distinct references.
`softwareco/agents/agent-principal` is the canonical persona/artifact repository for
that actor; it is neither the actor's identity issuer nor an appointment registry.

A persona revision or model/provider change changes configuration/provenance, not
actor identity automatically. An owner identity-replacement act creates a distinct
actor regardless of matching role, prompt or memory. Moving/cloning the repository
cannot appoint or duplicate the actor. The existing creator/executor's acting-authority
binding is separate from the intended Principal artifact/role-subject binding. These
roles may concern the same already identified actor only when its creator authority
is independently current; future/intended appointment supplies no permission.
Creator authority is checked at birth, not invented or required from an unborn subject. The owner binds the intended
artifact/role association before birth and confirms actual actor identity before
appointment/activation. [Identity rules](contracts-r3.md#identity-and-d24)
state the conditions and exceptions.

Holding D24's roles-with-memory direction remains authoritative. R3 retains R2's proposed
operational bridge using a **named role-bearing individual**, not role-type equality.
This is not an adopted interpretation of core.Role. Governance/semantic owner
confirmation of that D24 bridge is required before actor appointment/activation;
a rejected mapping requires revision, not company unilateral reinterpretation.

### P02 — concrete standing technical rights

Once a current appointment/delegation and target-owner admission cover the act, the
Principal may initiate, scope, claim, prioritize, coordinate/delegate, implement,
verify and follow through on **routine classes R0–R3** in the [decision-rights matrix](contracts-r3.md#decision-rights).
No new CEO approval is required for each such task. Routine closure requires the
target's done contract and explicit delegated lifecycle right.

Technical direction means selecting tactics and technical order **inside accepted
outcome, capacity and compatibility envelopes**. It does not mean accepting a new
shared architecture, reallocating another owner's commitment, adopting a new product
posture or changing another owner's decision. Those are X1–X5 reserved classes.
A self-authored task establishes scope/accountability, not new permission.

### P03 — reserved acts, current admission and ambiguity

Classification uses owner policy and evidenced impact, never a caller-supplied
“routine” label. The more restrictive applicable owner constraint wins; a substantive
owner disagreement cannot be cleared by convenient fallback or memory. Reversibility
alone does not make a live deployment, shared-interface change or security/privacy
change routine. Specific examples and refusal routes are in the contract appendix.

Reserved acts require current applicable owner authorization. A specific existing
standing release/service policy may admit its named actions; the Principal charter
itself grants none of those permissions. No new CEO checkpoint is required merely
for proposing a reserved action; performing it needs the relevant grant.

The [authority binding](contracts-r3.md#current-authority-binding) specifies the
minimum attributable facts and boundary outcomes: admit, denied, unavailable or
conflicting. Missing current evidence blocks the affected action. No unspecified
freshness cache, old approval, task number or role title may substitute for current
admission. Owner APIs/enforcers must be qualified before activation; this RFC does
not claim they already exist.

### P04 — continuity, replacement and review

A restarted session may continue as the same actor only after owner identity binding,
current appointment/permissions and fenced native recovery are established. Required
work/commitment references remain attributable; native AK session-bound task claims
are not transferred automatically. Same actor does not mean same active lease.

For a distinct successor actor, the owner separately decides appointment, allowed
memory access and each commitment's transfer/release/reconciliation. Until that
record exists, the successor cannot claim inherited responsibility or authority.

**Review date ≠ permission expiry ≠ execution lease.** A review becoming due creates
an accountable review obligation; permission expires then only if the issuer's terms
explicitly say so. Revocation, declared expiry and task leases keep their actual
owner semantics. No controller-invented 12-call, 60-minute or one-worker lifetime
restriction is introduced. Material unresolved review findings may trigger owner
suspension; they are not silently discarded.

### P05 — exception-level one-start inert-birth authority

After lawful template-owner acceptance, O3 issues one attributable D68 birth-exception
start right for the selected artifact/path/role/route. Its **issuer-owned canonical
right reference** is the unit of consumption. An execution permit from O6 references
that right; it cannot create, refresh or renew it. Permit/attempt/controller ID changes,
reference aliases, artifact cleanup or a lost reply cannot create another available
right under the same O3 disposition.

Bind actual creator/executor authority separately from intended artifact/role subject,
and bind accepted O3 record, exact task, source/pin/answers, destination/collision,
write scope, required native checks and validity/revocation. Prospective Principal
appointment, provider, memory and live effects remain excluded.

**Durable start consumes the parent right, across all child permits, before any birth
write.** The authoritative start receipt joins canonical right reference + O3 record
identity + permit + attempt + exact inputs + executor fence. O3 names its authoritative
consumption custodian; O6 may reserve/record only under that delegation. At most one
attempt can win that parent-level reservation. No default availability from a new
permit record or an absent receipt is allowed.

The [birth contract](contracts-r3.md#birth-state-contract) separates parent right state,
child permit/attempt state and artifact disposition. A known pre-start refusal consumes
nothing. Unknown start settlement holds ALL permits referencing that right; it is
neither known spent nor available authority. Confirmed start remains spent even after
failure, partial rollback, revocation, or artifact quarantine.

Same-attempt recovery needs confirmed original start/right association, fresh recovery
permission, old-executor fencing, identical bindings and a proved remaining step. It
creates zero new starts. A **new attempt** after spent/uncertain/revoked right requires
an explicit fresh applicable O3 template-owner exception/disposition, naming the old
right/attempt and their disposition and issuing a distinct right. O6 reissuance or
reusing the old O3 record is insufficient. Unestablished mandatory collision/fence/
effect conditions remain blockers even after a fresh owner decision. This is a new
reserved authorization act, not recurring birth power or a new full ADR mandated for
every technical recovery; the actual template-owner procedure controls its issuance.

Revocation blocks future admissions/writes at the actual owner boundary but proves
neither rollback nor absence of an artifact. Original history remains. The actual
parent-level reservation/custody/fence implementation must be qualified before birth;
no such native service or atomic cross-owner database is claimed here.

### P06 — target lineage, placement and compatibility

The admitted target is D167's company-pinned-L0 route A, with L1 configuration and
future explicitly admitted overlays. One canonical persona repository belongs to one
owner-identified actor; it records the exact role reference without becoming the
role/appointment authority. Native lineage, named placement-policy admission and
installed discovery/lint checks qualify this destination. Current source support
alone cannot pass them.

A source correction at a newer L0 commit cannot be silently used under the company's
old pin. The exact corrected commit and company pin/answers/adoption receipts must
join at birth. Legacy copy readers continue to support existing repos; migration
does not retroactively change old lineage or grant new-birth permission.

### P07 — rendered instructions and source conformance

Ordinary SoftwareCo work is main-first, as already required by company instructions
and D68's accepted RFC. Branch/worktree/PR use follows explicit review/isolation needs
and accepted repo-local contracts. No push/publication privilege follows.

Canonical template instructions must express stable defaults and actual owner-policy
lookup/configuration: unappointed is advisory; admitted standing authority is resolved
from owners; persona can narrow behavior but cannot issue a grant. Generated output
must match the selected company workflow and admitted placement. L0 source/tests,
AGENTS/CLAUDE loader preflight and governed company adoption repair conflicts; no
hand-edited generated override or uncontrolled arbitrary-policy configuration.

### P08 — memory stewardship, not data sovereignty

SoftwareCo-wide recall is the desired domain. Principal stewardship covers ordinary
relevance/compression/housekeeping inside current data-owner profiles. Scope, purpose,
lifetime, confidentiality, authority and retention are independent. Policy filtering
precedes retrieval/compression and provider release; withdrawal covers derivatives
and stale reingestion. Audit retention exceptions cannot regenerate withdrawn recall.

KES owns learning activation, Vault procedure dispatch, AK canonical authority and
original owners source/effect truth. OptMem/Souls inspire an independently authored
concept; no upstream reuse permission, private ingestion, storage/runtime/language
adoption or mission durability follows. Memory is a replaceable adapter, not the
mission executor or authority issuer.

### P09 — useful continued operation and distinct qualification

Routine blockers trigger causal investigation and correct owner handoff, not a new
CEO questionnaire for every tactic. Reserved authority gaps block only the dependent
act; useful unaffected work continues. Status-only indefinite waiting fails the
accepted result-or-blocker progress observation. No unconditional eventual success
is promised under unavailable owners/providers.

Inert birth, active host and memory are separately qualified. Complete M0 requires
real cognition/disclosure/confinement, native intent/choice/result/custody recovery
across result-commit-before-render, and accepted/rejected disposition recovery across
commit-before-export. Types, summaries, fixture checks or policy approval cannot
pass those runtime duties. Unknown effects require owner reconciliation; caller
cancel, admission withdrawal, physical submission and owner acknowledgment remain
distinct. One executor is required, with fenced takeover under its declared failure
profile.

## 6. Verification, migration and rollback acceptance

The [V01–V09 map](contracts-r3.md#clause-verification) assigns every clause an executable
obligation, accountable issuer and evidence receiver. These are acceptance contracts,
not claims that tests/enforcers passed. [Policy fixtures](policy-fixtures-r3.json) are
synthetic input/oracle cases; static consistency checks verify fixture construction,
not deployed authority or behavior.

Before ADR: actual RFC inspection; owner composition/decision rights and D24 bridge
condition accepted or explicitly revised; no unsupported owner/API claim. Before
issue/birth/activation: the corresponding real binding and V-case results. The ADR
need not wait for host or private-memory implementation, but cannot waive these later
conditions. A paper checklist is not a passing verification result.

Rollback is phase-specific: before birth, revert isolated source/adoption changes
under their owners and recheck pin/lineage; after partial/complete birth, revoke
unused/current action rights, retain artifact and evidence inert, reconcile effects,
and use owner-admitted repair/quarantine/disposal. Do not delete an agent repository,
destroy history or infer inactivity merely to obtain a clean retry. Existing fleet
and company-owned/agent-owned bytes remain unchanged except under exact admission.
Actual tasks, commands and release receipts belong to post-ADR owner scopes.

## 7. Options and owner questions

Rejected: perpetual advisory-only operation after an admitted appointment; persona-
granted power; blanket D68 supersession; mandatory whole-fleet/platform completion;
and downstream instruction edits hiding source defects. Route B is a legitimate
bounded compatibility option but is not silently activated. Its comparison against
A is explicit, not a strawman of permanent company copies.

Remaining **real owner decisions**, not generic architecture preferences:
- Confirm/reject the D24 named-role-bearing-individual bridge and exact role reference.
- Establish the current company/template/fleet authority acts and scopes in the
  owner matrix; resolve a substantive objection rather than impersonate its owner.
- Issue actual appointment, permit and data/host profiles only when their described
  bindings/checks are available. Missing enforcer support stays a qualification gap.

The [four current reviewer answers](reviewer-answers-r3.md) give concrete recommendations
and conditions through Guizzardi/Binner/Kasser/Parnas/Leveson confrontation. R2's earlier
five answers are preserved as history.
No school-of-thought argument substitutes for those owner acts or empirical results.
