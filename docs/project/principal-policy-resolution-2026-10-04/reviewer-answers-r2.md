# Five reviewer answers — many-of-the-greats

Subject: [R2 RFC](resolution-rfc-r2.md) and [contract appendix](contracts-r2.md).
This supporting reasoning is not a competing policy or an expert endorsement.
The author applied freshly checked text-safe many-of-the-greats v3, first-principles
v1 and second-order v1 in SoftwareCo context. These names confer no owner authority.

## Mode 1 — strongest schools and governing premises

### Category and identity correctness — Giancarlo Guizzardi

An actor, role type, appointment, session, authenticated principal, artifact,
permission, commitment and evidence are different categories. Meaning before syntax:
a label or a journal cannot fill a relation with a missing issuer or subject. If
role equality identifies every occupant as one actor, responsibility and authority
become untraceable. Reference, bridge and operational layers must preserve the
actual distinctions, not just use similar names.

Strongest case: owner-issued individual identity and role appointment govern
continuity; session/configuration changes are occurrences/revisions. Memory can
support a named role-bearing individual's continuity, never issue appointment or
transfer another actor's commitments. Core semantic adoption stays with its owner.

### Congruence and process accountability — Prof. Hartmut F. Binner

Responsibility without competence/decision rights is a broken delegation. Unbounded
technical power with no receiver or resource/custody responsibility is equally broken.
Führung sets mission; Input arranges resources; Transformation executes; Output
checks results; Leitung judges effectiveness/improves. Five segments are not four
process levels or software layers. The Principal can perform several responsibilities
without absorbing the human's reserved decisions or each source owner's accountability.

Strongest case: grant standing tactical/operative initiative within accepted mission,
capacity and interfaces. The CEO should receive consequential decisions and outcomes,
not every routine engineering question. A one-task-at-a-time permission robot cannot
own the technical result the operator expects.

### Receiver fitness and scientific discriminator — Joseph Kasser

The correct decomposition is the smallest **complete useful mission**, not the fewest
lines of code or the most complete platform. Holistic viewpoints expose interface,
time, resource and receiver assumptions. Scientific thinking asks which observation
would falsify the preferred contract. Source confidence, semantic sophistication and
beautiful diagrams do not establish actual receiver usefulness.

Strongest case: qualify one birth boundary and real source-only M0 while leaving
unnecessary host/memory/effect ambitions outside the first proof. Preserve exception,
rejection and uncertainty rather than force success. Do not add a new framework or
human bottleneck unless it closes a consequential observed gap.

### Safe control under partial failure — Nancy Leveson

A control action can be unsafe because it is issued by the wrong authority, at the
wrong time or after stale state—not because its local function is malformed. Admission,
execution/effect and acknowledgment are different dimensions. An interruption or
revocation does not prove physical rollback.

Strongest case: consume birth admission before writes, retain unknown effects and
prevent repeat admission without owner disposition. Atomic owner boundary/fencing
is indispensable; a pure transition chart or task-existence check cannot supply it.

### Information hiding and complete-cost delivery — Parnas, Brooks, Ashby/Beer

Hide volatile owner-record/runtime/render choices behind narrow adapters, rather
than baking a library or a second permission store into stable policy. Coordination
must represent actual partial/unknown outcomes, but essential variety is not a
license for infinite architecture work. Count complete receiver/maintenance duties,
not framework features or activity volume.

Strongest case: use the already accepted D167 route unless a bounded compatibility
alternative demonstrably changes receiver value and total qualification cost. Do
not qualify two birth paths or four speculative language prototypes by default.

## Mode 3 — concrete answers and contextual dominance

### Q1. What authority allows proceeding without another CEO checkpoint?

**Answer:** a current owner-issued appointment/delegation covering the identified
actor, role, jurisdiction and R0–R3 rights, joined with an exact active target task
and the target owner's applicable standing policy/profile at actual admission.
The charter permits routine initiation/scoping/delegation/implementation and allowed
closure; it does not itself grant every resource or reserved effect.

R1 docs/tests or implementation preserving accepted contracts can proceed without
another CEO action. A live gate change, new shared interface, disclosure expansion
or another owner's capacity displacement cannot be disguised as R1. An explicit
current standing service/release policy may admit its specified effects without a
new per-action human signature; the Principal title does not create that policy.

The owner-binding facts and admit/denied/unavailable/conflicting outcomes are in
P02/P03 and the appendix. Actual grant/read/enforcement surfaces are not presumed
implemented. Native schema 46 reports newer authorization records unavailable; this
proposal cannot invent a grant API or use legacy consent_mode as permission.

**Deciding clash:** Binner requires enough routine power to own outcomes; Guizzardi
requires a real issuer/relation; Leveson requires current boundary enforcement.

### Q2. Who decides when cross-repo work becomes reserved architecture?

**Answer:** applicable source/domain architecture authorities under SoftwareCo's
Tier-1 rules, with the relevant human decider for authority/architecture-significant
acceptance. The Principal may assess impact with evidence where routine assessment
is delegated; it cannot define itself exempt from those rules.

Coordination or execution of a previously accepted interface inside admitted envelopes
can be R1/R2. Deciding a new shared invariant/API/schema or breaking an accepted
compatibility commitment is X1. Displacing another owner's commitment/capacity is
X3. One small reversible diff can also be X2/X4 if it changes live permission or
deployment. Any applicable reserved trigger requires its owner's current authority.
Conflicting classifications are retained; material unknown impact is X5 and goes to
the relevant maintainer/owner, not automatically to the CEO for every small task.

**Deciding clash:** source-owner technical facts and Leveson's impact/timing dominate
caller labels; Binner keeps ordinary coordination inside standing agency. Parnas
keeps the rule in owner adapters rather than a new central Principal approvals queue.

### Q3. Does interrupted birth consume authorization; what permits recovery?

**Answer:** **yes, if durable atomic start admission occurred.** The permit admits
one attempt and loses its available start right before any birth write. Completed
records the accepted inert result; it is not the consumption point. A proved pre-
start refusal consumes nothing. If whether start happened is unknown, reconcile;
absence of reply is not proof of availability or nonexecution.

Owner-admitted recovery continues the SAME attempt only with current permission,
exclusive fencing, identical subject/path/code/pin/answers and trustworthy phase/
effect evidence establishing the remaining writes. No blind Copier overwrite,
new-ID retry or reset-to-available is allowed. If the next safe step cannot be proved,
retain uncertain_partial and obtain a distinct owner repair/disposal/new-attempt
admission that explicitly dispositions the original obligation.

Revoked permission blocks new writes but leaves artifact facts intact. A complete
artifact discovered after revocation remains historical/inert evidence, not newly
revived permission. The actual atomic/fence/recovery adapter must pass native proof;
a fake or static state graph cannot establish it.

**Deciding clash:** Leveson's no-repeat-under-uncertainty dominates retry convenience;
Kasser permits truthful partial/rejected outcome and narrowly useful recovery rather
than forcing successful generation at any cost.

### Q4. Which owner decides identity continuity after revision or replacement?

**Answer:** the accepted appointing/identity owner determines individual continuity;
governance/semantic owner confirms the D24 bridge and role meaning. Repository or
model maintainers record configuration revisions, not actor replacement by themselves.

Persona/model/provider revisions preserve the actor reference unless an actual owner
identity/replacement act says otherwise. They still require compatibility and any
changed disclosure qualification. New session occurrence is distinct, even for the
same actor; native session-bound task claims/leases do not transfer automatically.
A distinctly appointed successor receives separate appointment and per-obligation/
memory transfer disposition. Same role/prompt/history is insufficient.

R2 chooses a named role-bearing individual as the operational bridge, while explicitly
requiring its semantic owner's confirmation before appointment/activation. It does
not claim to have adopted or overridden Holding D24 or core.Role semantics. If that
owner rejects the bridge, revise before activation.

**Deciding clash:** Guizzardi's categories dominate identity; Binner's succession/
accountability ensures commitments do not disappear; Kasser prevents a copied persona
being advertised as qualified continuity.

### Q5. Why choose this birth route over the strongest bounded alternative?

**Answer:** route A qualifies the already accepted D167 pinned-L0 path, records its
company configuration/lineage once and avoids proving a second birth/refresh lifecycle
for the same actor. It also makes source-main-first/placement correction explicit.
AK6350's completed lineage work is reused; all-company copy removal is not required.

The serious alternative is route B: one exact legacy-copy birth under separately
admitted D68 exception, pin-equivalence proof, legacy-reader support and a refresh/
retention contract. D167's additive rollback compatibility gives B a plausible
technical basis, but does not issue a new Principal birth grant. B can be preferable
if A cannot yet produce a receiver-valued qualified result and B's complete admission/
maintenance proof has materially lower cost. No numerical speed evidence exists.

R2 therefore defaults to A, retains B as dormant, and requires an exact route-specific
owner disposition before B activates. Neither speculative urgency nor completion of
unrelated fleet cleanup chooses the route. No permanent copy ownership is reintroduced.

**Deciding clash:** Parnas/Brooks favor reducing total supported paths; Kasser allows
bounded compatibility when it changes real receiver value; Leveson/Guizzardi prevent
an expedient route from silently changing authority or evidence strength.

## Practical consequence and second-order checks

Do the contracted ordinary work without a new human signature when current standing
rights already cover it; stop only the dependent reserved/unknown effect. Issue no
birth until exact owner/pin/permit/fence conditions exist. Preserve original identities,
uncertain attempts and refused work products. Qualify runtime/data profiles separately.

Hypotheses to watch: more Principal initiative increases owner/custody load; shorter
summaries conceal exceptions; flexible template policy becomes an override system;
interactive code changes reinterpret retained state. Counterbalance with source-bound
classification, clause-specific real V proofs, receiver refusal and complete-duty
cost—not task counts, uptime or invented short operating caps.

These answers are explicit recommendations and testable boundaries. Remaining owner
confirmations and empirical results are named conditions, not author-fabricated facts.
No simulated school, document check or self-inspection supplies them.
