# Principal policy conflict: proposed settlement

## 1. Decision requested

Adopt the nine clauses in §5 through the owner-native Tier-1 decision process.
**Recommended settlement:** a standing, owner-appointed Principal with routine
technical initiative; a narrow, conditional principal-only birth exception to
D68; D167's pinned-L0 birth path preserved; main-first ordinary work; no persona-
created authority or implicit private-data/provider/effect access.

This RFC resolves the conceptual conflict and states the exact proposed change.
AK Decision **173** registers it as company-scoped `review_pending`, with no
review outcome, accepted outcome, ADR or legal review closure on initial readback.
Re-read the native passport before acting; these bytes are not a live state store.
It does not itself lift the freeze, appoint an actor, create an agent or authorize
source implementation. [Evidence and acceptance](evidence-and-acceptance.md) binds
sources, falsifiers and remaining owner actions. General lifecycle is owned by
[governance-kernel](../../../../holdingco/governance-kernel/docs/dev/decision-lifecycle.md).

The three required intellectual references are **Giancarlo Guizzardi**, **Prof.
Hartmut F. Binner** and **Joseph Kasser**. Their work informs the reasoning below;
no consultation, simulated vote or endorsement is claimed. Parnas, Leveson,
Ashby/Beer and Brooks contribute only where they change a consequential choice.

## 3. Many-of-the-greats — strongest schools

These interpretations confront incompatible assumptions rather than collecting
names. Native SoftwareCo-scoped Vault check returned text_ok for
many-of-the-greats v3, first-principles v1, root-cause v1 and second-order v1.

### Guizzardi: identity, categories and authority

Strongest claim: if the categories are wrong, apparent agreement is false. An
enduring actor, role type, role appointment, session occurrence and authenticated
security principal cannot be substituted for one another. A stewardship relation
says whom the actor serves; a permission permits an action; a commitment is an
obligation to a receiver; a description/evidence record asserts a fact.

Premises: identity persists independently of a current process; authority has an
issuer and scope; history and present validity differ. Strongest case: replacing
a session, matching a prompt or restoring a journal cannot renew a revoked grant
or transfer a predecessor's obligations by accident.

What this school catches: “Principal” and “agent” are being used to mean artifact,
actor, appointment and execution. Distinguish them before changing a directive.
Existing D24 anticipates role-with-memory continuity, but does not operationalize
this actor's appointment. Keep that distinction without rewriting the Holding
Owner's semantic decision from this company RFC.

### Binner: accountability and congruent technical ownership

Strongest claim: tasks, competence and responsibility must match. Delegating
outcomes while withholding routine sequencing/resources/implementation choices
creates responsibility without power; granting broad power without accountable
output creates the opposite failure.

Premises: Führung sets mission, Input provides resources/information, Transformation
executes, Output checks result/custody, Leitung judges effectiveness and improves.
The five segments are not software layers or the four process levels. Strategic,
tactical, dispositive and operative owners remain distinct even when one actor
performs more than one responsibility.

Strongest case: a standing Principal should initiate and steer useful technical
work in an accepted domain. CEO-retained mission/appointment and source-owner
acceptance are compatible with that initiative. Requiring the CEO to approve each
ordinary task recreates the bottleneck the Principal is intended to remove.

What this school catches: “safety” can be used to justify indefinite human routing
for every technical detail; “autonomy” can hide unowned capacity, support or custody.
Neither is a balanced operating process.

### Kasser: receiver fitness and scientific falsifiers

Strongest claim: the system exists to complete a useful receiver mission, not to
maximize formal distinctions, scenarios or policy artifacts. Receiver acceptance
must judge whether technical judgment and continuity improve the actual decision.

Premises: operational, structural, temporal and quantitative viewpoints expose
interfaces and drift; scientific perspective asks what could falsify the proposal.
Strongest case: prove one template-readiness decision and one complete source-only
mission. Do not demand universal memory or a live XENO bridge to create a useful
inert actor scaffold; do not pretend a compiled prompt completes the mission.

What this school catches: “everything is blocked until the whole platform exists”
is false completeness; “the compiler is green” is false validation.

### Safety/control and information hiding: Leveson and Parnas

Strongest claim: unsafe control often comes from incorrect authority/timing, not
broken local computation. Hide volatile template/runtime decisions behind explicit
owner seams; let the final effect owner check current permission.

Premises: named guards, supervisors, tools and memory do not prove effect authority;
interfaces should hide the decisions likely to change. Strongest case: one mission
executor, separate effect/disclosure boundaries and versioned source/appointment
references. Avoid a second authority database or stacked schedulers.

What this school catches: a newly broad persona can bypass a still-advisory runtime,
or a restart can repeat an uncertain operation; neither is cured by a role title.

### Coordination and delivery: Ashby/Beer and Brooks

Strongest claim: autonomous initiative must represent the variety of real outcomes
without making the operator manually coordinate every event. Essential uncertainty,
refusal, conflicting evidence and unavailable owners cannot be simplified away.
But scaffolding a governance apparatus before one useful outcome adds accidental
complexity rather than requisite coordination.

What this school catches: fake productivity through infinite retries, endless
questionnaires, role inflation or indefinite “ready” dashboards.

## 4. Confrontation and adjudication

### Clash A: authority correctness vs initiative

Fundamental contradiction: treating advice as authority versus treating every
routine action as human-reserved. Both cannot meet the intended mandate.

**Settlement:** keep unappointed posture advisory; accepted owner appointment
supplies standing authority. Principal initiates routine technical tasks inside
that domain without per-task CEO signoff. Self-authored tasks scope execution but
cannot create reserved permissions. This preserves Guizzardi's category boundary
and Binner's task/competence/responsibility congruence.

### Clash B: frozen production vs receiver-first delivery

Fundamental contradiction: new production birth without the required decision vs
indefinite delay for unrelated host/memory/effect capabilities.

**Settlement:** explicit conditional exception for this exact inert principal,
not blanket cancellation of D68. Required source/pin/role/collision/birth evidence
comes first. Host activation, private memory and live effects remain separate.
Authority correctness dominates the birth effect; Kasser's useful thread dominates
what investigation and preparation can continue now.

### Clash C: policy as boilerplate vs policy as current owner state

Fixed branch/path text is simple to render but cannot correctly represent company
configuration and standing appointment. Unrestricted prompt overrides are simpler
still and even less truthful.

**Settlement:** template-owned instructions state stable defaults, authority lookup
and admitted company configuration. Persona contributes behavior and narrower
scope; it cannot appoint or override owner policy. Source owners repair their
instructions/tests; exact company/actor bindings stay owner-native.

### Clash D: company memory reuse vs source-owner authority/privacy

“Within SoftwareCo” is useful jurisdiction, but treating it as uniform access or
truth conflicts with data-owner rights and KES learning activation.

**Settlement:** Principal is memory steward within admitted profiles, not owner of
all information. Current access/disclosure/withdrawal is checked before retrieval,
compression and release. Recalled decisions/procedures are qualified references;
current AK/Vault/ROCS/KES owners retain their concerns. No language choice supplies
these guarantees.

### Chosen path

**Contextual dominance**, not fake universal synthesis. Standing technical
initiative dominates routine admitted work. Owner-issued authority and safety
constraints dominate boundary expansion and effects. Receiver fitness dominates
qualification priorities. Source information hiding dominates implementation form.
Unresolved empirical runtime/language questions remain experiments, not policy.

## 5. Exact proposed clauses

### P01 — identity and appointment

The Principal is one standing actor serving the operator through SoftwareCo under
an owner-issued appointment to an exact canonical System Architect role reference,
displayed as Principal Architect. Actor, role, appointment, session and security
principal references remain distinct. This decision establishes the charter;
it does not appoint an unidentified actor or synthesize an appointment record.

### P02 — standing technical initiative

Once appointment and jurisdiction are admitted, the Principal may discover issues,
set technical direction within accepted product/domain commitments, prioritize,
initiate/scope/claim/coordinate/delegate tasks, implement, verify and follow through
within admitted SoftwareCo owned/infra/contrib/fork/agent domains and source-owner
contracts. Allowed routine task closure follows the actual done contract and
standing lifecycle delegation. No new CEO approval is required for every routine
task or relevance/compression choice.

The charter's review obligation is not a short execution epoch or an automatic
expiry invented by the controller. Actual appointment validity/review/revocation
must be supplied by its owner record. Existing source-owner resource controls remain;
no 12-call, 60-minute or one-worker permanent budget is introduced.

### P03 — retained authority

The operator retains mission/business intent, appointment/replacement/revocation,
new material business commitments, authority/jurisdiction expansion and unresolved
company/cross-owner risk decisions. Privacy/disclosure, licensing/security relief,
irreversible/high-blast-radius actions, release/publication/external effects and
source-owner reserved acceptances require the applicable current owner authority.
The Principal is not CTO, residual company owner, publisher, AK administrator or
XENO privilege holder by title. A scope-valid task is not a loophole around this.

### P04 — actor continuity and commitments

Session replacement for the same owner-identified actor preserves attributable
work/commitment lineage only through admitted recovery and current authorization.
Appointing a distinct replacement actor requires owner disposition of accessible
memory and transferred/released obligations. Prompt, role or journal equality does
not decide actor identity. Expired task leases and revoked permission remain owner
facts; recalled approval cannot revive them.

### P05 — exact birth exception to D68

After this clause is lawfully accepted by the relevant company/template authority,
D68's new-production-L2 freeze is modified **only** to permit one inert birth at
`softwareco/agents/agent-principal`, under an exact semantically admitted creation
task, qualified source lineage/pin, accepted role/name/collision checks, substantive
six-input persona, current compiled prompt and required native birth evidence.

All other new-L2/non-canary rollout restrictions remain. Birth does not activate
provider/tools/memory, grant appointment permissions, adopt the host/language,
remove company copies, publish or authorize XENO. Failure to establish a required
condition blocks the birth; no timeout, task number or compiler pass waives it.

This is one-use birth authority, consumed by the admitted birth and immediately
withdrawable by its issuer. The owner must record review/validity terms before
issue. A changed actor/role/destination/source pin or an unresolved partial birth
requires explicit owner disposition, not automatic repetition. The exception is
not a recurring fleet-generation permission or a short operating budget for the
future Principal.

### P06 — template lineage and location

D167 remains the accepted target: L0 templates rendered for the company at the
company's admitted pin; L1 supplies configuration and future admitted overlays,
not permanent copies. The actor destination is the selected SoftwareCo agent path,
with one standalone L2 per actor/artifact and an exact role binding. Native lineage,
placement-policy and installed discovery checks remain required.

AK6351 is the existing birth-path implementation carrier, not the agent-creation
permission. Do not make completion of copy removals across every company a birth
prerequisite once the exact birth path is qualified. No transitional copy fallback
is silently authorized by this RFC; any necessary alternative needs its own exact
owner disposition with pin-equivalence evidence and later refresh compatibility.

### P07 — ordinary workflow and template conformance

SoftwareCo ordinary work is main-first, as already stated by company instructions
and D68's accepted RFC. Branch/worktree/PR use is for explicit review or isolation
needs and accepted repo-local contracts; no push/publication right is inferred.
Template instructions must not impose contradictory universal branch/MR-only policy
or a fixed fleet home that defeats admitted company placement. L0 authoring, tests
and governed company adoption repair the defect; future generated AGENTS is not
hand-edited to manufacture an override. AGENTS/CLAUDE loader preflight precedes any
such instruction mutation.

### P08 — memory stewardship

Desired memory jurisdiction is SoftwareCo cross-project/company work. Principal
stewardship governs ordinary relevance, summarization and qualified housekeeping
within admitted profiles. Scope, purpose, persistence, confidentiality, authority
and retention remain independent. Current policy filters before recall/compression
and provider disclosure; withdrawal propagates to derivatives and stale reingestion.
KES retains learning promotion, Vault procedure governance, AK canonical authority,
and original source/effect owners their evidence. Owned OptMem/Souls-inspired design
is permitted direction, not upstream-code reuse, private-data access, runtime/language
adoption or implemented durability. Actual implementations need exact owner tasks.

### P09 — long-running operation and receiver proof

Standing mandate permits continued useful work through ordinary technical blockers,
including causal investigation and bounded owner handoffs, rather than repeated
routine permission questionnaires. Missing reserved authority blocks only the affected
action; useful unaffected discovery may continue. Honest blocked/unknown states are
not a completed outcome, and status-only indefinite waiting does not pass progress.

A qualified long-running host needs one executor, retained intent/choices/results/
dispositions, custody, fenced replacement and current access/effect admission. Prove
complete M0 with both crash cuts and receiver acceptance/refusal before expanding
runtime claims. Caller cancellation is not admission withdrawal, submission rollback
or owner acknowledgment. Unknown effects require reconciliation, never blind retry.

## 6. Alternatives rejected and reversal conditions

- **Keep all activity advisory forever:** preserves default safety but contradicts
  the receiver's standing outcome mandate. Use only while unappointed.
- **Treat persona/user enthusiasm as full authority:** fast-looking, category-wrong,
  bypasses freeze/data/effect owners. Reject regardless of language elegance.
- **Supersede all D68/CTO policy:** too broad; would erase unrelated retained owner
  constraints and imply CTO delegation. Use narrowly scoped P05 instead.
- **Require entire fleet/memory/host migration before inert birth:** no demonstrated
  necessary dependency. Require only the exact birth path and its boundary proofs.
- **Hand-edit this agent's instructions or install OptMem to get moving:** hides the
  source defect or substitutes memory for identity/permission. Reject.

Reconsider the narrow birth exception if its owner cannot establish a safe supported
birth/refresh lineage, a semantic role collision is material, or the receiver no
longer values the proposed Principal. Change runtime/memory tactics when matched
complete-duty evidence reverses the current hypotheses, not to evade the clauses.

## 7. Root cause and second-level consequences

Observed symptoms: source instructions contradict selected/main-first needs; syntax
checks can pass while semantic creation authority remains absent. Current shared
source parity is good. Thus “old L1 drift alone” is insufficient as root explanation.
D68 already expected main-first output: introducing main-first again as a new waiver
would misdiagnose a conformance failure as a policy conflict.

Five whys: wrong generated posture → fixed source prose → presence checks not current
policy conformance → source/template tests did not reflect the active receiver/owner
contract → deeper historical reason **unknown**. Stop at the actionable mechanism,
not fabricated blame. Detection fix: actual consumer birth fixtures for selected
company posture, absent appointment/inert behavior and semantic refusal cases.

Second-order hypotheses: standing initiative increases useful flow but also owner
admission/support load; reusable memory lowers reconstruction but can hide stale
exceptions; configurable instruction policy eases placement but can become an
uncontrolled override system. Third-order risk is self-improvement that enlarges
mandate through memory/persona or task authoring.

Balance by observing useful receiver results and complete-duty ownership cost,
keeping reserved authority at current owner boundaries and preserving inspectable
rejections/counterexamples. Do not reward task count, uptime, green dashboards or
short summaries at the expense of truthful qualifications. No imaginary production
SLA or controller-invented lifetime cap is used as a substitute for this accountability.
