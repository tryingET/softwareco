# R3 in plain terms

**Recommendation: approve R3 as the ADR basis.** This does not adopt the policy,
appoint a Principal, lift a freeze or authorize birth. The [fresh inspection](inspection-memo.md)
found no material proposal defect after reading the [frozen RFC](../resolution-rfc-r3.md),
[contract](../contracts-r3.md) and [integrated answers](../reviewer-answers-r3.md).

Compact terminology:
- **O3 issuer:** the actual template authority that grants the one-start exception.
- **Consumption custodian:** the one owner-designated authority that records and
  reports whether that canonical right has started. O6 can hold this role only by
  explicit O3 delegation; custody does not grant renewal power.
- **O6 execution owner:** admits/fences execution and its permits within its scope.
- **E / P / T / A:** canonical start right / execution permit / original attempt /
  resulting artifact. New P or a cleaned A cannot make old spent E unused.

## QUESTION

Does R3 give one useful birth a defensible one-start boundary and recoverable
continuity, or does it merely rename tokens while allowing another start?

## MODE 1 — MANY OF THE GREATS

### School 1: identity and authority categories — Guizzardi

- **Core claim:** a new execution token is not a new permission.
- **Premises:** policy, authorization act, permission, executor, intended subject,
  permit, attempt and physical artifact have different identity conditions.
- **Strongest case:** if the one-use unit is P, a second P can validly consume its
  own local budget and still violate the owner's one-birth exception. Only E,
  resolved by its issuer across aliases, can preserve the intended restriction.
- **What it sees:** actor sameness and technical token uniqueness cannot establish
  identity of the authority act. Same person in O3/O6 capacities is not one merged act.

### School 2: task–competence–responsibility congruence — Prof. Binner

- **Core claim:** an outcome mandate without attributable competence and custody
  is dysfunctional organisation design; so is making every routine tactic an owner ceremony.
- **Premises:** authority belongs to actual process owners; execution and feedback
  need named receivers. Führung sets goals, Input provisions competence/resources,
  Transformation does work, Output checks results, Leitung judges feedback.
- **Strongest case:** the Principal must complete admitted routine outcomes without
  repeated CEO approval. O3 controls exception issuance; the delegated custodian/O6
  carries consumption/execution responsibility. Original safe completion is not
  another policy initiative; replacement authority remains reserved.
- **What it sees:** a single human can hold both offices while separate records
  preserve accountability. Fragmented approvals with no responsible receiver fail too.

### School 3: useful complete outcomes and falsification — Joseph Kasser

- **Core claim:** a precise boundary must deliver a receiver-valued result, not
  reward document volume, synthetic pass counts or indefinite waiting.
- **Premises:** internal consistency and actual fitness are different claims.
  Tests must threaten the claim they purport to check, and conditions must be staged.
- **Strongest case:** different permits sharing E, post-partial reissue and original-
  attempt recovery are the decisive challenges. Same-token locking is too weak.
  A qualified original remaining step preserves useful progress; building a universal
  permissions platform first imposes unsubstantiated opportunity cost.
- **What it sees:** accepting a conditional ADR can be sound while actual birth/M0
  remains unqualified. Fixture success cannot establish receiver acceptance or no effect.

### School 4: unsafe control under partial failure — Leveson

- **Core claim:** uncertainty cannot create available authority.
- **Premises:** lost replies, revoked permissions and physical writes are different
  observations; competing controllers and delays are part of the failure environment.
- **Strongest case:** consume E before writes, hold all sibling permits if settlement
  is unknown, and fence the old executor before recovery. Cleanup cannot erase history.
- **What it sees:** a locally tidy retry can be a globally unsafe second start.

### School 5: source-owned information hiding — Parnas

- **Core claim:** specify the boundary contract without absorbing its implementation.
- **Premises:** issuer and custodian retain their own authoritative mechanisms;
  logical fields are not a second canonical store or an implemented API.
- **Strongest case:** one qualified owner adapter can supply E identity, state and
  receipt custody. A universal cross-owner transaction is unnecessary and expands risk.
- **What it sees:** architectural precision can coexist with implementation deferral;
  it cannot coexist with pretending an unavailable native endpoint is shipped.

## MODE 2 — CONFRONTATION

### Clash 1: identity/control safety versus token-level convenience

- **Fundamental contradiction:** each permit is independently one-use versus one
  exception across all permits. Both cannot govern the same O3 disposition.
- **Incompatible assumptions:** changing P renews start power versus the issuer's E
  remains spent. The first permits two conformant local starts that violate the owner rule.
- **Identity/safety explains better:** alias, race and post-cleanup second-start hazards.
- **Convenience explains better:** simple local execution bookkeeping, but only below E.
- **Residual tension:** the actual custodian/alias/reservation adapter still needs proof;
  naming it in policy does not deploy it. R3 chooses E as authoritative.

### Clash 2: complete useful recovery versus issuer-controlled replacement

- **Fundamental contradiction:** every interruption is a new reserved birth versus
  any repeat with the same T label is merely continuation. Neither extreme is sound.
- **Incompatible assumptions:** the label proves continuity versus boundary evidence
  proves original E/P/T, fence, unchanged inputs and a known remaining step.
- **Binner/Kasser explain better:** finishing the originally admitted result without
  another whole ADR when its complete recovery contract holds.
- **Guizzardi/Leveson explain better:** detecting a different or blind start hidden
  under the old label and keeping unknown settlement from becoming permission.
- **Residual tension:** conservative holds can cost useful progress. R3 assigns that
  reserved disposition to O3 rather than giving O6 implicit renewal power.

### Clash 3: exact evidence versus universal-platform prerequisites

- **Fundamental contradiction:** no activation without boundary evidence versus
  no policy decision until every host/memory/authority platform is complete.
- **Incompatible assumptions:** a required fact must be empirically established at
  its effect stage versus all future facts must already exist before a conditional ADR.
- **Kasser/Parnas explain better:** stage-specific useful qualification, hidden adapters
  and no gratuitous language/storage/platform redesign.
- **Safety explains better:** refusing a dependent birth/action profile when actual
  evidence is unavailable, regardless of architectural elegance.
- **Residual tension:** the native newer authorization query is unavailable on schema
  46. That limits evidence through that surface; it does not prove all owner records absent.

## MODE 3 — INTEGRATION OR DECISION

**Chosen path: contextual dominance.** Category correctness and safe control dominate
new-start admission. Process-owner congruence and receiver value favor original
recovery only when its evidence contract holds. Information hiding dominates adapter
design: qualify a real owner boundary, do not fabricate another authority platform.
